## Method notes

- **Scope:** Cross-platform search of Chinese and English professional articles, official docs, YouTube, Bilibili, Reddit and GitHub. 40 techniques and 28 resource entries. This is a curated library, not an exhaustive list or a ranking by likes.
- **How to choose:** For narrative and dialogue, start with hard cut, cut on action and J/L-cuts. For travel or product footage, look at match cuts, whip pans, occlusion and speed ramps. For motion graphics, look at wipes and pushes. Consider generative bridges only when bridging footage is missing.
- **Using the prompts:** Replace A and B with clip names or shot descriptions, then copy the prompt. Name the actual software or model. Editing AI must be able to edit a timeline; code AI must render code; generative video AI must accept the relevant reference images.
- **About the parameters:** All suggested durations, detailed steps and prompts are editorial suggestions based on the sources, not verbatim instructions from the original authors, and have not been tested one by one on specific AI models. Seconds are a starting point for a test cut; adjust for pace and frame rate.
- **Source mapping:** Source IDs in the main table map to the sources table. The main source provides the technical concept or implementation basis; additional sources may be viewing entry points. Entries with only search snippets or unavailable videos are noted.
- **About popularity figures:** High-engagement original tutorials that could be verified are prioritised. YouTube figures come from search-index snapshots and may lag by about two months; GitHub stars come from the API on the retrieval date. Likes, views and stars are not combined into a score.
- **Blank values:** Engagement figures that are not public, not obtained or not reliably verifiable are left blank. Channel subscribers, total account likes and third-party estimates are not used as single-video likes.
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-11-15. This is when sources were consulted; it doesn't mean every page or figure was updated that day.
- **Source quality:** Techniques follow official docs, creator tutorials and professional articles first. Community posts are used to discover terms and problems. High likes show reach but don't by themselves prove a method suits your footage.
- **AI resource types:** Remotion skills and the SKILL.md in GL Transitions are agent guidance resources. The GLSL library, FFmpeg filters and research papers are code resources, render tools and research methods respectively, and should not all be treated as installable skills.
- **Footage prep:** Leave enough handles on both sides of a transition. Shots to be matched should share motion direction, subject position, exposure and white balance where possible. Sound transitions need the audio tracks checked; picture effects don't replace them.
- **Limits of generative video:** Generative prompts require selecting and trimming results; body, text and identity may drift. Do frame-accurate timing, subtitles, logos and A/V sync in an editing or code tool first.
